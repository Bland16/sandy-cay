The day-note header line

design/DAY-NOTES.md §4 · six treatments, at the three real column widths

Day notes now render (DayNotes.jsx, one component, three surfaces). What the suite cannot see is whether the line reads at the widths it actually has to live in — jsdom has no layout, so truncation, crowding and the multi-day band are all unproven. That is the whole question here.

Every column below is a real width taken from the app: 150px is a desktop day, 110px is a tablet weekday (Mon–Fri sharing the room the weekend gave up), and 74px is the phone's week overview — that number comes straight out of .grid{min-width:calc(46px + var(--cols)*74px)}, so it is the narrowest the line ever has to survive.

The week is Mon 23 – Sun 29 Nov 2026, and Thanksgiving 2026 genuinely is Thursday 26. The hard cases are deliberate: Thu has three notes (one with a long institutional label), Thanksgiving spans Wed–Fri, Make-a-thon spans the weekend, and Mon is clear — an ordinary day header must keep the shape it has always had.

Nothing here is coral, and no treatment tints the column. A holiday is a fact about the day, not a scheduling problem (P-1). A tint means blocked, which is D-6's model change and the next item — previewed at the bottom so you can see the two do not collide.

If you want a recommendation

A is what shipped, and at 150px and 110px it is fine. At 74px the pennant costs about a character and a half of label, which is why B exists. D is the one worth looking at hardest: §4 says a multi-day note should read "as a band across the week rather than a mark on its first day", and repeating the label on each of Wed/Thu/Fri is the weaker reading of that sentence — D draws it once, continuous, and is the only treatment where "Thanksgiving is three days long" is visible rather than inferred. It costs a real layout change (a band row spanning grid columns, which the weekend drawer would have to be taught, since a band from Friday into Saturday is cut in half by the drawer's edge) — so it is a genuine trade, not a free upgrade. C is the loudest and starts to read as a state rather than an annotation, which is the line D-1 drew. F loses the most characters to its own border.
