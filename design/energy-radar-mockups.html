This is worth doing, and here is the argument for it. The card in the Cabana today shows each axis as "steady" — a single net number. Net is what the engine acts on, but net throws away the thing you most want to see: a day that spends 6 and restores 6 nets to zero and reads identical to a day where nothing happened at all. Overlaying the two recovers exactly that difference, which is why your instinct is right.

It costs no model change. Every task already carries a four-axis load and a duration, so spend and restore per axis are a few lines over data the app already has: L[a] > 0 → spend[a] += L[a]×hours, L[a] < 0 → restore[a] += −L[a]×hours, and net = spend − restore, which is what energyBudget already reports.

One honest limit, stated up front. loadForTask already merges a task's own positive and negative contributions per axis before returning (posMean + negMean). So this split is "hours that spent you" versus "hours that gave back" — not a full double-entry ledger of a single task doing both. It is the true shape of the day at task granularity, and it should not be described as more than that.

Four axes, not six. Your reference gets a pleasing hexagon; four points give a diamond, which is coarser and distorts more — two axes high and two low reads as a slash. That is the one real cost, and it is why the butterfly below is here as a rival rather than as a consolation. Numbers are printed on every version regardless, exactly as your reference prints 169/169 — §10 forbids meaning carried by colour alone, and a polygon with no numerals is precisely that.

Which one

The radar is the better card, the butterfly is the better report. The radar wins where you want shape recognition at a glance — the Cabana's "Energy today", where you look at it daily and learn what your own Tuesday looks like. Its weakness is real though: four axes distort, and two overlapping translucent polygons are exactly the kind of thing that dies on a greyscale printer, which the wrap report is designed to survive.

The butterfly wins in the report — it is exact, it reads without decoding, it prints, and it scales to a week without turning into spaghetti. If only one gets built, build the butterfly, because it can live in both places; the radar can only live in one.

A capacity ring is the thing to be careful with. A dashed ring showing what you sustain is the most useful mark on the chart — and it is exactly what P-2 forbids inventing. learnedCapacity returns null until roughly three weeks of ratings, and you are at 0 ratings, cold start. So the honest version has no ring at all yet, and says so. The last card below shows both states side by side, because a chart that quietly grows a ceiling later is a chart that lied earlier.
