P1The “When” field

Today the panel asks for a day of the week, and silently means the week you are currently looking at. That is why adding a September event from an August view is impossible without navigating there first. The fix is a real date, plus a readback that tells you what day of the week it lands on — because a bare 2026-09-03 does not tell you it is a Thursday, and that is what you are actually thinking in.

Why the readback. You will be typing dates months out. “Thursday · 3 weeks ahead” catches the fat-fingered year and the wrong-month slip before you submit, which a bare ISO date cannot.

Why “more options” is collapsed. Your call — the wider placement range is real but rare, so the panel stays one field for the common case and opens for the case that needs it.

Why the toast changed. Once a date can be outside the viewed week, a task can land somewhere you cannot see. The toast names the date and offers to take you there rather than letting it vanish.

P2Monthly, ordinal, yearly

The frequency list currently tops out at “every 4th week”, and every row is a weekday. “The first Tuesday”, “the 15th” and “every September 3rd” are all inexpressible — which is also why importing a calendar loses them.

There are only two kinds of monthly, and one is far more common. “The first Tuesday” keeps the weekday and moves the date (1 Sep, 6 Oct, 3 Nov) — that is meetings, clubs, classes. “The 15th” keeps the date and moves the weekday — that is rent and bills. Rather than make you choose between those two ideas in the abstract, the editor writes both options out as finished sentences from the date you already picked, and shows the real dates underneath. Change the date on the right and watch every option rewrite itself.

Why no “mode” picker. The first draft of this mockup asked you to choose “on a date” or “on the …” before showing you anything, and that was confusing — rightly. You should never have to hold the by-date / by-position distinction in your head. Picking a date already tells us both answers, so the editor just offers them as sentences and lets the preview settle it.

The rule that still needs your eye: months that don’t have the date. Set the date to 31 October and pick “every month on the 31st” — the preview says it skips November, February and April, because clamping to the 30th would invent a session on a date you never chose (this is also what RFC 5545 does). “The last day” and “the last Tuesday” are offered precisely because they always fire. You find this out in the panel, not in November.

P3The import that loses your events

This one is not a missing feature, it is silent data loss. ical.js:84 drops any RRULE that is not FREQ=WEEKLY — but the event still imports, just without its pattern. Proven by probe against a four-event calendar on 2026-08-11:

Event in the .icsPatternWhat Sandy Cay stored
Rent dueFREQ=MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY=1one event, 1 Sep, no repeat
Book clubFREQ=MONTHLY;BYDAY=3TUone event, 15 Sep, no repeat
Mum’s birthdayFREQ=YEARLYone event, 3 Sep, no repeat
LectureFREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO,WEkept correctly

P2 makes the first three importable. But some RRULEs will still be unreadable (FREQ=DAILY, BYWEEKNO), so import must also report what it flattened instead of staying quiet. That half is worth shipping even on its own:
